# Master vision

## Product identity

Bladefall should become a compact, distinctive single-player metroidvania-like
action-adventure rather than a rapid showcase of every mechanic. It should keep
its faster original DNA—speedrunners can still move quickly—but first-time
players should inhabit a coherent dream world, meet people, infer history,
explore meaningful branches, return with new abilities, and learn bosses through
practice.

The Hollow Knight/Silksong comparison is about the *rewarding structure*, not
copying their tone or bulk. Bladefall should be a smaller game with fewer,
better-connected systems, a more direct portal identity, stronger environmental
causality, and a military Datura-delirium narrative.

## Narrative truth

The player is a real knight poisoned at night by enemy soldiers. The poison is
Datura, communicated visually through white trumpet flowers but not named in
ordinary player-facing text. The game world is the knight's anticholinergic
delirium: ordinary soldiers, officers, commanders, objects, routines, and bodily
symptoms become distorted landscapes, enemies, generals, mechanisms, and laws.

The mystery must be inferable without being announced:

- the opening camp is interrupted and wrong;
- clocks independently repeat 3:40 and advance only very slowly on returns;
- Ethereal Goods calls its stock the knight's existing “effects”;
- flowers, cup, bandage, wristguard, command token, missing faces, pulse skips,
  painful light, and real-world military correspondences recur;
- early memories are undiagnostic; after three or four, a careful player can
  form the theory;
- the Void Tyrant/second-to-last command-chain revelation confirms the dream and
  the real officer/right-hand correspondence;
- the knight concludes that defeating the final ruler may be the only way to wake.

NPCs should have occupations, regional concerns, stateful dialogue, and visible
relationships to place. Environmental evidence should carry more story than
large text boxes. Dialogue should be concise, anchored to its source, disappear
when irrelevant, and replay only through deliberate Up interaction.

## Endings

Both endings share the same real-world opening after the final battle: the
knight wakes delirious, enemy soldiers break into the tent, and he mounts a
credible but impaired defense.

### Direct ending

Without completing the Deep Line truth route, the hallucination fails him. He
fights halfway decently but real blades kill him. The game resolves to: **“He did
not wake again.”**

### Deep Line ending

If the player completed the Gilded Vault and Deep Line, a flameblade becomes the
focus at the moment of defeat. Dream imagery—portals, fire, remembered power—
surges back. The knight seizes the apparent flameblade and kills the attackers,
then collapses. The camera reveals that the weapon is only an ordinary rusty
axe, leaving open whether delirium, memory, or something stranger helped him.
After darkness, a seeming comrade says exactly: **“He who saved us has awoken.”**
The knight slowly opens his eyes and the game ends.

## World topology

The sixteen existing stages are persistent places on one connected map, not a
playlist. Travel occurs through tunnels, roads, holes, climbs, doors, conduits,
fissures, and rails—not completion portals.

The intended itinerary deliberately doubles back:

1. Outskirts → Black Woods → Broken Causeway.
2. Return to Black Woods; Dash opens the route upward into Updrafts.
3. Updrafts → Hollow Marksman → Ruined Keep.
4. Wall Jump and the Keep Key force a meaningful westward return through prior
   regions to the Outskirts western breach.
5. Outskirts → Warden → Frostfell.
6. Return through Warden/Outskirts/Black Woods/Brute; Double Jump and Wall Jump
   open the high shaft toward Frost Sorcerer.
7. Frost Sorcerer → Emberdeep → Ember Colossus → Inversion → Void Tyrant →
   Abyss King.
8. The direct ending follows the King. Seven recovered Vault Keys instead open
   Gilded Vault → Deep Line and the truth ending.

Fast travel may target only discovered safe anchors and cannot discover a
forward node. Backtracking must contain new routes, quests, memories, named
rewards, keys, or shortcuts—not merely longer empty walking.

## Progressive ability sequence

The knight starts with one jump and no weapon. Missing inputs should simply do
nothing; “memory missing” spam is not part of the desired experience.

| Order | Ability | Earned in | Design purpose |
| ---: | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Jump | Outskirts | Single-jump mastery and unarmed evasion |
| 2 | Weapon | Black Woods | Oathblade ceremony and first authored combat |
| 3 | Dash | Broken Causeway | Opens bruised roots and faster commitments |
| 4 | Linked Portal | Updrafts | One player mouth plus authored counterpart |
| 5 | Twin Portals | Hollow Marksman | Player owns both mouths |
| 6 | Wall Jump | Ruined Keep | Immediate Archive proof and world-scale return |
| 7 | Guard / Counter | Warden | First active lesson belongs to Frostfell |
| 8 | Double Jump | Frostfell | Reopens earlier high routes and high shaft |
| 9 | Attunement | Frost Sorcerer | Unifies elemental/environment interactions |
| 10 | Companion Command | Emberdeep | Makes a follower an intentional puzzle actor |
| 11 | Downward Strike | Ember Colossus | Combat-movement verb and foundry breach |
| 12 | Gravity Flip | Inversion | Full floor/ceiling spatial language |

New abilities should be acquired at the end of one chapter or in a protected
midpoint, safely demonstrated, then used more deeply by the next chapter.

## Portal design constitution

Portals are Bladefall's signature, but repetition weakens them. Every major
puzzle must change the noun, goal, or consequence—not simply add a second copy
of a previous fling.

- Early: fixed links and one movable mouth.
- Then: both player-owned mouths, decoy surfaces with honest consequences, and
  the realization that the correct orientation matters.
- Later uses include routing hostile shots, carrying architecture, redirecting
  airflow, crossing a guarded facing, preserving warmth, passing a traveler
  through a selective barrier, cooling a molten projectile, combining with
  gravity, rebuilding across enemy body heights, and surviving portal hijack.
- Bosses must remain mobile threats. A mouth cannot become permanent safe cover.
- Decoys should be plausible and informative, not arbitrary traps or labeled
  “wrong” answers.
- Payloads need broad receivers and automatic recall. A wall-edge collision must
  never consume the only solution object.
- Players should sometimes infer that both mouths are theirs rather than receive
  a large instruction panel.
- Reset, death, checkpoint, revisit, and co-op snapshots must agree on mouth,
  mechanism, and gate state.

## Compact reward/build economy

The game should use a few understandable reward families:

1. **Permanent memories** — traversal/combat verbs above; never unequipped.
2. **Weapon and Mantle** — named physical equipment. `R` picks up; the first
   weapon auto-equips; `I` opens the bag. Armor visibly changes the knight.
3. **Gift** — one major rule modifier, independent of costume. Costumes are
   cosmetic trophies. Fractional lifesteal was rejected; Blood Vow is a discrete
   retaliation rule compatible with whole Blood.
4. **Echoes** — small charm-like modifiers, weighted capacity, limited equipped
   forms, changed only at refuges. Their effects should interact with existing
   verbs and environments rather than add isolated minigames.
5. **Advancement** — four Blood fragments form one permanent Blood Knot; finite
   Forge Seals authorize meaningful equipment work.

Gold remains shop currency. Anonymous loot carpets, random stat choices, XP
skill screens, duplicate crafting economies, and pickup prompts for every loose
object are contrary to the current direction. Optional branches should award a
named piece, Echo, Gift, fragment, Seal, key, correspondence, quest change, or
sealed Recollection.

## Blood, damage, and defense

Health is communicated as discrete Blood reserves—approximately five meaningful
hits, not a long fractional health bar. A hit normally costs one whole measure.
Armor/Ward or later authored effects can bank enough protection to increase the
number of survivable hits to six, seven, or eight without turning the HUD back
into opaque arithmetic. Boss and enemy health bars are hidden when conventional
bars would undermine learning through behavior and phase language.

## Recollections and the preserved original game

Every campaign level should hide one Sealed Recollection. The bag shows found
entries with a padlock and makes clear that a Waking Key exists at the end of the
game. Before that key, the recollections cannot be played.

After the ending, the Waking Key opens isolated versions of the original
pre-reimagining stages: the faster game where the knight had the whole kit from
the start. These should be redesigned as darker, harder challenge memories that
preserve the old game's identity without contaminating campaign saves,
progression, leaderboards, or world state.

## Pacing and authored-space rules

- Each region should take roughly 25–45 minutes for a first-time player and
  retain a deterministic 4–12 minute practiced line.
- A room teaches one local rule, tests it, combines it once, then gives the
  player room to breathe.
- Shops begin after the basics, in safe inhabited places.
- Every platform, fluid, machine, hazard, item, NPC, and portal surface needs a
  visible support and a reason to occupy that location.
- Water must be contained by a basin; wind must originate in vents, pressure,
  bellows, or weather; floating platform clutter is not authored geography.
- Enemies should use local terrain and fields, pursue beyond tiny patrol strips,
  commit to attacks, and recover from lethal world pits. The environment may
  soften ordinary enemies but should not kill the encounter offscreen.
- Critical, optional, return, and speed routes must remain readable without
  floating solution text.
- Checkpoints should be sparse, intentional, attractive, and tied to meaningful
  retry boundaries—not placed side by side.

## Audio/presentation direction

- A level keeps one exploration cue through ordinary checkpoints unless an
  authored boss or major persistent world-event cue begins.
- Pause/map surfaces preserve the current level cue.
- The opening text uses the Outskirts music quietly and fades into normal level
  volume; it does not show literal story art that spoils the mystery.
- Current assigned music includes Strange Worlds (Outskirts), light exploration
  tracks such as Sunlight Through Leaves/Floating Dream/Drifting Memories,
  Whispering Woods for Warden exploration, ClockWork for activated Frostfell,
  Element/Abnormal Circumstances/Heat of Battle for selected bosses, Tides of
  the Serpent for the second-to-last boss when integrated, and the original
  `music.mp3` for the final boss.
- Environmental SFX should be material-aware and restrained. Repetitive chainmail
  landing audio is reserved for meaningful heavy landings.

## Co-op and NG+ boundary

Co-op and remix systems already exist, but the agreed production order is:

1. finish and human-validate the entire single-player Base journey;
2. adapt co-op so players, weapons/projectiles, enemy deaths, per-player loot,
   shared mechanisms, travelers, and transitions remain authoritative;
3. redesign NG+1 and NG+2 as meaningful rule-changing versions rather than
   simple numerical difficulty.

Existing NG+ environmental laws and the NG+2 Linewalker/second-follower reward
are useful prototypes, not permission to skip a dedicated future pass.


## Current extension: the Muster return

Frostfell now demonstrates Counter, environmental heat routing and Double Jump,
then tests Double Jump on exposed ice before an optional deliberate engine
interaction changes its atmosphere and local enemies. The military recall is a
world-story extension, not a replacement progression constitution. Since
2026-09-11 the recall installs authored rosters in Warden, Outskirts, Black Woods
and Broken Causeway; the return's rewards and later regions remain planned. The
owner explicitly prefers surprise, without advance hints warning of the escalation.

The broader return should change occupation, enemy roles, access and rewards,
preserve solved progress and avoid reviving bosses. See
[recent plans](11-RECENT-CHANGES-AND-PLANS.md) for the boundary between implementation
and proposal. Ancillary Gemini suggestions inform review but cannot override
ability order, optional-equipment freedom or already accepted level teaching.
