# Fractured Causeway: retired studies and three proposed puzzles

Historical proposal: these three designs are now implemented. See [current design and verification](FRACTURED-CAUSEWAY-THREE-PUZZLES.md). The original proposal and its pre-build cautions follow.

Original status: eight early studies are removed from the active chapter and native editor. The Line Behind the Brittle Wall remains unchanged as a draft, not a finished approved room. All three designs below are proposals only. No proposed room, geometry revision, art, voice or runtime mechanic has been implemented.

## Design diagnosis

The retired studies mainly demonstrate movement properties. Away teaches exit direction/refill; Keeps Speed and Downhill choose between two lines; Runs Home teaches one-way entry. Sill Clock, Opens on the Beat and Lift Keeps the Beat chiefly schedule an arrival. Keep Weight Off introduces a useful enemy but makes the weight an action to avoid, rather than a tool with another necessary role.

These can be readable mechanic introductions without supplying a developed spatial puzzle. Selecting the useful line usually completes the reasoning, and waiting for another cycle can replace preparation. A blocked alternative and a required mechanic establish route dependence; they do not establish an interesting deduction. Increasing spike density or narrowing the timing window would not supply the missing relationships.

The retained draft is stronger because the brute changes navigable space by breaking the wall protecting the line mouth. Its current safe reading ledge makes that preparation largely passive: the existing ordinary-input witnesses clear with waits of 160, 200, 240 and 300 frames. Afterward, the line and returned dash resolve the exit. Preserve the readable enemy-as-tool relationship, but add a consequential second decision.

The intended standard for replacements is a visible goal, an initially unsuitable arrangement, a useful preparation, and a later movement payoff that depends on that preparation. An object may change roles. Failure should explain the arrangement; routine waiting and narrow reflex windows should not be the principal source of difficulty.

## 1. Revise The Line Behind the Brittle Wall: one brute, two openings

Keep the right-hand wall, the line behind it, and the brute. Add a second brittle wall on the opposite side of the chamber. Its tall column blocks the upper finishing corridor, while its base remains on the brute's running floor.

Sequence:

1. Use the first bait position to send the brute into the right wall, exposing the line mouth.
2. Move deliberately so the brute commits away from the newly opened alcove. Enter the line during that committed charge, rather than waiting indefinitely in one safe place.
3. The line now leads to an upper staging perch, not directly to the finish. From this new position, bait the same brute back left. Its charge breaks the second wall at the base, opening the high corridor through the same column.
4. Use the opened upper route for a short, demanding final transfer. A safe standing area should let the player assess the second arrangement before committing.

The deduction is that the brute must be kept available and directed twice. Opening the first wall changes access, not victory. The line is a way to reach a new bait position, not merely an exit ride.

Before building: the second bait position must actually be within the existing brute's range and line of sight; initial scenery must prevent reaching it without the freed line. The two walls must not open in one charge. Existing scenery and collision must enforce these facts, not a hidden stage counter. The entry into the alcove should have a generous readable charge/recovery interval. If the second perch can be reached directly or both walls can be handled from the starting ledge, the proposed puzzle has not been achieved.

## 2. New: The Weight You Must Take Back

Use a lantern, a recoil platform, a low connecting passage and a high exit. At first, the lantern is needed as a persistent weight while the player leaves the platform's vicinity and approaches from the other side.

Sequence:

1. Place the lantern on the recoil platform. Its lowered position opens the useful low route through the machinery.
2. Leave the lantern there and take that route around to the platform's boarding side. Carrying it with you too early lets the platform rise and removes the needed passage.
3. Board the depressed platform and take the lantern back while holding the platform down with the knight's own weight.
4. Throw the lantern clear onto a permanent shelf, release the platform and take its upward launch. Make the final dash empty-handed through the high opening.

The same lantern is first the thing that preserves preparation and then the load that must be removed. The useful change is a transfer of responsibility from object to player, followed by a launch. This is more than choosing how long to wait for a lift.

Before building: rule out simply boarding from the entrance and charging with the knight, jumping straight to the far approach, or removing the lantern and still crossing before the platform moves. The throw shelf must keep a missed attempt recoverable. Throwing/releasing/jumping must work with a comfortable input window using the existing lift grace; do not make a tiny pickup/throw trick the puzzle. The exact geometry and input margin remain unproven until implemented and measured.

## 3. New: The Wall You Must Fold After Climbing

Use a hanging hinged arm, an upper line mouth and an initially missing receiving runway. The arm's upper pivot is accessible from the upper ledge. Its initial downward pose is useful as a climbing wall; the next leftward pose supplies the later landing.

Sequence:

1. Climb the hanging arm to reach its upper ledge and pivot. Folding it before the climb removes the way up.
2. From the top, fold the arm horizontally toward the entrance. It stops being the wall just climbed and becomes a receiving shelf beneath the line's return trajectory.
3. Reach the line mouth from the upper perch and ride back toward the newly prepared shelf.
4. Land on the arm, then use its runway for an airborne down-diagonal landing dash and wavedash beneath the canopy. Finish with the remaining aerial transfer.

The deduction is to use the structure in one pose before preparing its second role for a landing that has not happened yet. Returning on the line without preparing the shelf leaves no useful runway. There is no blinking-mouth clock.

Before building: the pivot must be reachable from the upper ledge, and the proposed turn must be unobstructed. The upper mouth perch must not provide an equally useful launch. Direct line-exit dashes, ordinary jumps, ground boosts from the entrance and attempts to stand on the canopy must be checked. The receiving arm must matter physically; an arbitrary required-turn flag would not solve the design problem.

The eight retired definitions, their chapter sections, required-room entries and reference tapes are absent. The native chapter is regenerated. The retained room is now index 37, so the existing launcher opens it. Chapter 23 has 38 definitions and 14 required challenges.

The retained definition and recording are unchanged, as are all other room layouts except the crossroads' incoming connection metadata. Only the retained room and crossroads move to close the removed stretch of road. Shared physics, rewards, quests, other chapters and the browser game are unchanged.
