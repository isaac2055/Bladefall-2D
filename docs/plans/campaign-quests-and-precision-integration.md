# Campaign quests and precision-platforming integration

Planning proposal, 27 September 2026. No gameplay implementation authorized or performed for this plan.

## Recommendation

Make the new movement devices part of the world's working machinery and geography. Use them to cross the main road, reach settlements, repair equipment and expose shortcuts. Base regions retain exploration, combat and quieter human encounters. Fractured regions develop the same movement rules into demanding, repeatable sequences with precise timing and few resting places.

Cover the 20 active base regions and their 20 Fractured counterparts. The cut stage stays cut. The two standalone precision courses remain practice and regression spaces; the Cloaked Man remains a separate boss project. Do not invent a new level or promise an Echo boss implementation under this plan.

The first two quest refreshes remain the standard for writing and staging, not a template to copy twenty times. The remaining scope includes 18 regional stories, six quest-ledger entries, existing traveler encounters and Act Two's central water/colony story.

## Evidence and constraints from the current build

- `public/bladefall-celeste.js` restricts its new devices to stages 41/42 and supplies a fixed course kit. Its progression shutters, consumed-device receipts and retry behavior are course assumptions, not campaign requirements.
- `public/bladefall-campaign.js` lists the active base/Fractured regions and room identities. Existing level charters and runtime modules take precedence over stale coordinates in older plans.
- `public/bladefall-side-stories.js` contains 20 regional stories. `bladefall-side-story-world.js` still places the 18 later stories around a refuge with a generic short stair. Some actions have real prerequisites already: linked flow, Echo contacts, a loaded rescue cart, escorts, directional strikes and the alarm sequence. Preserve those working rules while replacing the generic geography and outcomes.
- `bladefall-quests.js` adds six ledger quests; these overlap with residents and campaign mechanisms. Integrate them rather than building a competing second set of objectives.
- Ability order: jump → weapon (Woods) → dash (Causeway) → single linked portal (Updrafts) → independent pair (Marksman) → wall jump (Keep) → counter (Warden) → double jump (Frostfell) → attunement (Sorcerer) → companion command (Emberdeep) → downward strike (Colossus) → gravity (Inversion) → Chainhook (Thunder Cliffs). Placement must respect the actual pickup within each region, not simply its stage number.
- The Updrafts charter awards its portal at the exit. Its cartographer currently requires that ability: the proposed portal task therefore belongs at a reachable post-unlock terrace, with a shortcut back to the desk. No portal-dependent gate on the first ascent.
- `echoVerbAvailable()` gates the Tyrant's Echo to its late encounter/cleared state. The Figure's release must be explicitly staged after that availability, with a return connection; no early request for an unavailable action.
- Thunder Cliffs grants the hook through authored windlass/encounter progression. The sailors' upper-rigging task belongs after the first real grant, regardless of the catalog's general description of the boss reward.
- Existing Inversion has an intentionally difficult, floorless serpentine finish. Preserve that requested challenge; base-game integration does not imply flattening the game's existing peaks.
- Existing Tyrant routes already use required zipline momentum and portal transfers. Refine these as anchors of the integration, avoiding parallel shortcut geometry that makes the line unnecessary.

Celeste's useful precedent is precision with generous interpretation of intent. Maddy Thorson's [Celeste & Forgiveness](https://www.mattmakesgames.com/articles/celeste_and_forgiveness/index.html) describes buffered jumps, ledge grace, corner correction and briefly retained lift momentum. These support hard geometry; they are not reasons to remove it. The proposals below are Bladefall designs, not claims about Celeste's exact level construction.

## Movement vocabulary

| Device | Bladefall expression and consistent rule | Base application | Fractured application |
|---|---|---|---|
| Momentum carrier | Freight hoist, chain cradle, ice elevator or tide float. Jump inherits its travel velocity; a brief visible arrival charge expires and is spent by jumping. | One clear takeoff and generous destination, followed by a later transfer. | Jump near arrival, steer around a lip, then connect to the next device before landing. |
| Tension release | Existing rideable lines and later Chainhook rings. A release launches along a readable trajectory. Clearly distinguish automatic line riding from the acquired hook. | Carry the player across a real broken span; a safe perch lets them inspect the next step. | Early/late release changes whether the player clears a ceiling or reaches the catch. Waiting is safe only at designated hanging rings. |
| Rebound anvil | Spring-loaded forge plate or stone striker. Holding Down commits one pound per hold; release rearms it. | Introduce after downward strike is acquired. One impact visibly compresses and launches. | Convert a fall into an ascent, then dash or flip through an opening. Avoid traps where continued Down immediately destroys the rebound. |
| Blade targets | Hinged strike plates: downward targets and upward latches with distinct silhouettes and arrow marks. Direction + attack accepts either input order. | A safe environmental blade lesson after obtaining the sword; one target opens a higher route. | Alternate downward and upward strikes with air steering and dash conservation. |
| Aether form | Compact luminous shard shaped around the knight's crest/blade, retaining a recognizable core. Smooth four-direction steering, curved turns, brake, deliberate dash exit and finite charge. | One wind-channel detour in Updrafts, then selective returns in magical regions. | Curved corridors require both steering and speed control, a necessary refill, then an aimed exit into another mechanic. |
| Portal/tear transfer | Ordinary portals rotate/preserve momentum under one consistent rule. Specifically marked launch mouths provide an obvious extra burst; do not silently boost every portal. | Teach a launch across a visible gap, then introduce an exit direction change. | Exit trajectory must feed a blade target, carrier or gravity corridor. A transfer is a purposeful part of the motion. |
| Gravity integration | Keep gravity as an airborne steering tool as well as a way to change surfaces. Inversion sources and later polarity rings have distinct marks. | Pair a flip with a moving surface, a portal exit or a ledge catch. | Floorless curved weaving interrupted by target strikes, portal exits and controlled catches. |

Environmental blade targets should work with the sword without equipping Pure Nail. This is a deliberate proposed extension of course behavior to marked world devices, not a free grant of enemy pogo or downward slam. Keep enemy-pogo gifting and Colossus's downward-strike unlock meaningful.

Aether and the jetpack have separate jobs. The jetpack remains thrust-and-gravity flight with a visible knight; aether is a temporary steering transformation. Do not replace the existing Needlewind jetpack route wholesale. First aether exposure should be a short parallel wind channel after the local flight lesson, with a safe entry and exit. Later portal-to-aether combinations are allowed only after the player has learned both parts.

Retain the course's current 0.18-second arrival charge and 0.12-second departure grace as the first lift prototype baseline. Start with the existing directional-attack chord tolerance and held-pound separation. Keep underlying handling consistent between halves; tune difficulty primarily through destination shape, speed, approach and combinations. Any timing changes require playtesting and one consistent visual cue.

## What makes the platforming necessary

Main-route crossings should physically require their intended movement. A long launch gap, obstructed upper route or deliberately oriented portal should explain the solution before the player tries it. No invisible gate asking whether a checklist of devices was touched.

Base: usually one mechanic plus one familiar action, progressing to two or three linked actions at a regional climax. Include a recovery shelf or nearby reset while teaching. Integrate into existing rooms instead of appending another long obstacle course to every level.

Fractured: typically three to five linked decisions in a short room, with selected longer finales. The player must choose when to jump, where to aim, whether to brake and when to spend the dash. Start prototype rooms at roughly 10–25 seconds of successful motion; these are pacing targets, not promised calibrated durations. Put the checkpoint immediately before the difficult sequence, with no replayed dialogue or long elevator wait.

Keep coyote time, input buffering and reasonable correction. Do not make difficulty depend on a one-frame button order, a tiny unreadable hitbox, variable equipment speed, or an offscreen hazard. Tune against the legal loadouts players can actually bring; optional speed upgrades must not make precision impossible. Prefer reliable device launch velocities and clear caps on device-granted momentum to silently overriding the whole campaign movement kit.

## Quest structure and presentation

A regional story gets a recognizable person, a practical place and two to four meaningful actions. Complexity comes from what the player changes, not from extra errands. A return should reveal a new route or a changed scene, often using a shortcut opened on the outward journey.

Keep one next objective visible, using a named landmark and a concrete action. Show the distant destination from the giver or an overlook when possible. Put information and talk choices on safe ground. Quest props differ from background through silhouette, motion and contrast; no constant cloud of labels. Let scaffolds, ropes, pipes and paths explain connections.

Every scene needs authored arrival, task and outcome performances, including refusals. Avoid teleporting residents between static poses. Longer optional conversations should add specific memories and motivations, not restate the task poetically. Most initial replies should fit two to four sentences. Descriptive endings can take one or two short paragraphs, but must describe what the player sees or learns, not actions the game never performs.

Do not make every resident emotionally confess before providing help. Some are practical, impatient, funny, private or interested in the job. Optional disagreement should have a believable consequence; it should not undo a rescued person, shut the campaign road or withhold a traversal-critical ability.

## Three sequences to prototype first

**Causeway, base.** The player sees the destination above a broken span. Freeing the actual catches brings the freight cradle up. Its strip lights near the stop. Jump while it is moving or briefly after arrival; the carried momentum clears the span. A broad maintenance shelf catches an imperfect landing. On the side return, a launch plus the newly learned dash reaches Silas's governor. The level's mechanical repair has taught a useful movement skill.

**Fractured Inversion.** Start on one safe ledge. Pound a rebound plate, flip beneath the first thorn arch, hit an upward latch, enter a prepared mouth sideways, emerge into a curved gravity weave and catch a hanging rest ring beyond it. The weave has no floor. A camera preview reveals the catch before takeoff. Retry restores the plate, portal arrangement, hazard phase and personal movement state together. Test that each link is necessary and the entire route works without damage boosting.

**Palace garden, base.** The gardener points to visibly sun-damaged beds. Closing the first shutter powers down the glare and exposes a carrier's landing; use it to reach the second handle. That closure shades a gallery and makes a tension release safe. The final launch reaches the highest shutter. The return descends along the newly safe garden path while flowers open below. The quest's changing environment is its platforming progression and its emotional outcome.

## Co-op and failure rules

Shared: enemies, puzzle mechanisms, physical quest repairs, NPC decisions and outcomes. Either player may solve a switch or retrieve a quest object once; both see its actual state. Required quest items have one shared world identity and an identified carrier, with safe recovery on disconnect.

Personal: movement, cameras, aether charge, dash refills, blade-target availability needed for traversal, momentum charges, attempts and checkpoint recovery. One player using a refill cannot strand the other. A moving lift can activate for both but must return or be callable, and must not consume the other rider's jump charge.

Both knights still traverse and arrive at campaign exits. Solving a shared puzzle should help the partner; finishing a movement room must not teleport them past it. Do not impose course-style per-device receipts on ordinary campaign paths. Important dialogue has a shared settled choice and replayable transcript, without freezing either player's network/world state.

Fractured room retries restore personal resources immediately. Shared machine/hazard state needs an explicit local retry policy that cannot rewind an actively traversing partner: use repeatable deterministic cycles and per-player contact availability, or coordinate a shared room reset only when neither player is mid-attempt. Never blindly apply the solo course retry to the whole co-op world.

Precision should not depend on the guest receiving a fresh packet on the exact jump frame. Predict local carrier contact and launch from synchronized trajectories, reconcile shared activations, and avoid transmitting every scenic object every frame. Validate with latency and jitter, not just an in-memory link.

## Implementation order, if approved later

1. **Movement foundation:** extract reusable device behavior from course-only state; retain the courses unchanged as reference routes. Separate devices, regional art, progression and reset policy. Consolidate input semantics, portal/velocity transformations and local co-op prediction.
2. **Representative prototypes:** Causeway carrier, Woods blade target, post-unlock Updrafts aether/portal terrace, and Fractured Inversion combination. Prove actual required motion before producing twenty variants.
3. **Base Act One:** refresh each regional quest alongside the level's authored integration, in acquisition order. Continue to protect boss rules, existing difficult sections and returning-player saves.
4. **Base Act Two:** work through rigging, tide, colony, mirror stair and light-powered palace. Tie optional scenes to existing world decisions.
5. **All Fractured regions:** give each its listed signature, then add supporting rooms and adjust the difficulty curve. Fractured has its own itinerary; verify its actual kit at every checkpoint rather than inheriting base unlock assumptions.
6. **Full polish and review:** character art and animation, dialogue accuracy, camera previews, touch/controller controls, co-op and save migration. Retain standalone courses for learning and regression, not as prerequisites.

## Acceptance before calling an eventual implementation finished

- Every listed region has a documented, played integration; every remaining regional story has authored geography and truthful stage directions. Existing ledger/traveler stories are audited too.
- Required launch routes fail when attempted without the intended momentum, across plausible jump/dash/portal alternatives; successful routes use legal inputs without position corrections, invulnerability or forced progress.
- Aether refills and gravity changes have demonstrated purposes. Blade hits connect physically; missed or wrong-direction attacks do not advance progress. Lift charge expires visibly and cannot be banked forever.
- Fresh campaign and Fractured starts reach each introduction with the needed abilities; later return quests are clearly labeled and reconnect without trapping the player.
- Every positive/refusal outcome is staged, not merely described. Branch prose follows actual events. Scenes remain readable at desktop and mobile sizes and in each renderer.
- Quest saves migrate without losing completed work or duplicating rewards. Retries restore the promised state; abandoned optional quests cannot block an exit.
- Two players can solve shared puzzles, see enemies/outcomes, traverse at different speeds, retry independently and reconnect without lost abilities or stolen pickups. Test real-network latency/jitter and measure frame time in effect-heavy rooms.
- Successful automated routes establish reachability, not fun. Human play reviews compare first-read clarity, fatigue, repeated failure causes and whether the player understands their next correction. Final timing and spacing are not approved merely because a bot passes.
