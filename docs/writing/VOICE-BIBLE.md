# Bladefall voice bible (2026-09-27)

Every spoken line in the game should pass one test: **cover the name and you can still tell who is
talking.** People talk about concrete things — a sore shoulder, the kettle, the patrols, what they
want from the knight. Nobody speaks in portents. Feeling comes through specifics and what is left
unsaid. Any line that carries gameplay information (where to go, what to stand on, what a switch
does) says it plainly, in that speaker's voice.

Scope: spoken lines only. Narration, signs, item text, tutorial prompts and quest-log objectives
are not dialogue and are not governed here.

**Fixed lines.** "He who saved us has awoken." (the true ending, `bladefall-cinematics.js`) is the
owner's agreed line and never changes. The white-flower sickness is never named, and nobody may
call it leprosy (`docs/plans/act-two.md`). The Cloaked Man (stage 43) does not speak; his closing
card credits the owner's little brother, and that card is not dialogue.

**Dialect.** Use it lightly and the same way every time: one or two markers in a line, never
phonetic spelling across a whole sentence. Each dialect marker belongs to one speaker only, as
listed below.

---

## The knight (the player's replies)

Role: a soldier with his memory gone, poisoned, in a tent (the player never learns this from him).
Where: every choice button.
- Few words. One clause if one will do.
- Three registers per choice set: **decent** (an offer or a thanks), **curious** (one direct
  question), **curt** (impatient or contemptuous, never cartoonish).
- No dialect and no jargon. He never explains himself and never makes speeches.
- He calls people nothing: no names, no "friend", no "sir".

> "I'll get it. Then we'll cross together."

## Act One residents and companions

### Mara · Road Surveyor
Where: the Outskirts (survey, field chart); Hollow Marksman (her mark on the watch token; the
quest actor still has the internal id `daro`).
- Clipped lists: counts, names, positions ("Ash, old camp. Glass, Watcher's Cut.").
- Survey words: bearing, marker, chart, reading, "the measurements close".
- Leads with the fact, gives the reason after it. Never hedges about a measurement.
- Brisk thanks, once. No sentiment about roads.

> "Three bearings for my chart. Ash, old camp. Glass, Watcher's Cut. Iron, Broken Muster—meet me there."

### Vey · Wounded Standard-Bearer, later keyholder and sluice keeper
Where: the Outskirts fire; the Warden's gaol (the keys); the White Court sluice.
- Long, looping sentences chained with "and", with dashes for the things he meant to do and hasn't.
- Domestic detail: kettle, blankets, bowls, where to sit.
- Plays down his wound with a joke. He mentions the standard only in passing.
- Ends on an offer: sit, rest, have the tea.
- Never gives orders. (Rowan says Vey "always makes too much" supper.)

> "Sit this side—the other blanket's still wet, and I keep meaning to hang it out."

### Olan · Road Keeper
Where: Watcher's Cut, the Outskirts (a quiet figure at present).
- Old countryman. Drops the g on one or two -ing words a line (*mornin'*, *crossin'*). He is the
  only speaker who does this.
- Says "reckon" and "mind". Slow and patient: he never hurries the knight.
- Talks about his knees, the stuck clock and the patrols.

> "Don't go trustin' that clock. Patrols still come through, mind."

### Orra · Clock Keeper
Where: the Black Woods refuge (a quiet figure at present).
- Wanders off the point (her daughter, who mended the watch), then comes back with "Anyway."
- Prickly when told something she already knows ("thank you").
- Northern grammar: "gave it me". Gets the directions right once she reaches them.

> "Yes, I wound it—same as every morning since my daughter gave it me. Anyway."

### Pell · Resin Worker
Where: the Black Woods (a quiet figure at present).
- A tradesman's rules of thumb, repeated like a chant: "Wind first, then amber."
- Trade words: windward, lee side, resin, bark.
- Makes fun of himself (the nettle patch). Imperatives aimed straight at the knight.

> "Good branch, the amber sits on the side facing the wind."

### Hale · Quiet Veteran
Where: the Black Woods, by the rooted sword (a quiet figure at present).
- Fragments with no subject: "Tried pulling it. Shoulder lost."
- Understatement and dry humour, with a soldier's plainness. Never talks about feelings.
- Refuses help in as few words as possible.

> "Sword in the roots up ahead. Tried pulling it. Shoulder lost."

### Bram · Lantern-Keeper (companion)
Where: the Black Woods, carrying the lamp.
- Eager and chatty. Exclamations, and tag questions ("right?").
- Calls the lamp "she". He is the only one who talks about a thing as "she".
- Owns up to being clumsy: "I jump like a sack of flour."
- Asks the knight to wait for him, and offers to wait in turn.

> "Stay close enough to see what she lights up, right?"

### Oren · Chainwright
Where: the Broken Causeway; the Updrafts (internal profile `ilyra`); the Ruined Keep; Emberdeep
and the Throne (internal profile `sera`); in Act Two, the kneeling conscript, the colony yard, the
Sun Gate and the Throne of Noon.
- Northern craftsman. "Aye", and "'ll not" / "'d let" contractions ("He'll not run this time").
  He is the only one who says "Aye".
- Trade words: load, strain, catch, cradle, links, stitch, stamp, grip. He hears machines by
  their noise.
- Short to medium sentences, practical kindness and dry jokes. In Emberdeep, under fifteen words
  a line (the region's charter).
- In Act Two, the same voice, worn thin: fewer words, and he tells no jokes until the cure.

> "Hear that groan? That's the gate chain taking the whole load on its own."

### Sable · Stretcher-Keeper, the colony's physician
Where: the Broken Causeway (a quiet figure at present); the Colony spring in Act Two. Sable is
"he".
- A calm bedside manner. Instructions come in doses: "Slowly. One spoon at a time."
- Says "we" about care, and is honest about the risks without drama.
- Short sentences, often imperative. He never raises his voice.
- Keeps "One thing at a time. Start there." as his signature.

> "Not by rushing. Sit where I can see you, and breathe."

### Talla · Kite-Mender
Where: Bellows Rest, the Updrafts (a quiet figure at present).
- Brisk and sharp. Sewing words: stitch, strap, seam, cloth.
- Gives orders in pairs ("take both"). Has little patience for tuggers.

> "Harness is tied off above the drafts. Brake pin's hanging beside it—take both."

### Edrin · Rain-Catcher Keeper
Where: the Rain-Catcher Span, the Updrafts (a quiet figure at present).
- A fussy, proud amateur engineer. Explains things at length, with "see?" and "you see".
- Possessive about "my cups". Hates waste.

> "Each gutter spills into the next one down, see, and the old cups tip when they're full."

### Senn · Veil-Mender
Where: the watching road, Hollow Marksman (a quiet figure at present).
- A sardonic seamstress. Dry one-liners and mild put-downs.
- Calls the portals "mouths" and knows which one is nailed down.

> "The orange mouth's nailed to the far scaffold. Doesn't move. The blue one's yours."

### Veya · Hearth Keeper
Where: the Ruined Keep gatehouse (a quiet figure at present).
- A tart innkeeper, weary of guests who never stay.
- Gives quick comebacks and clear directions (west gate, high archive).

> "The west gate's the only road out. East is just more Keep, and it isn't friendly."

### Nim · Frost Tender (companion)
Where: Frostfell, carrying the banked flame. No pronoun has been fixed for Nim.
- Young, solemn and literal. Short, simple sentences.
- Proud of the flame and will not be ordered about ("It's a flame, not a servant").
- Notices plain physical facts with a child's wonder (footprints that stop).

> "Your footprints stopped in the snow. Then they started again, higher up."

### Tovin · Keep Mason (escort, and the Keeper's Counter)
Where: the Ruined Keep.
- Big, warm and slow. Calls the knight "friend". He is the only one who does.
- Proud of stonework, and explains how things were built ("one to hold, one to pass").
- Laughs at himself ("Ha—").

> "These gates were built for two people, friend—one to hold, one to pass."

### Ada · Glassworker
Where: the White Court (a dormant line at present).
- Frank and irritable about the court. Craft words: molds, cooling.
- Adds a useful tip as an afterthought ("Oh—").

> "Dry molds crack, so I'm idle till Vey gets that wheel released."

### The wandering traveler (generic escort)
- Plain, tentative, and polite. Hedges with "I think".

> "There's a way through up ahead, I think. Walk with me a bit?"
