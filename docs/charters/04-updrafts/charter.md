## Full-success vision

The Updrafts is a windwright settlement built around one dangerous piece of
weather, not an aerial mechanics showroom. A first visit should take 18–26
minutes and alternate effort with stable places to land, look, speak, and infer
how the settlement works. A practiced player keeps a deterministic 4–6 minute
line, but cannot fly over the final deduction or use a checkpoint to discover a
later world.

The chapter's language is **capture, carry, land, redirect**. The borrowed Aerie
Harness makes height temporary because fuel is restored by deliberate landings.
Thermals are positioned where bellows, vents, sails, or canyon pressure explain
them. The same air moves the player, walking enemies, elemental reactions, and
finally the Signal Crown. The portal finale therefore asks the player to redirect
an environmental force, not fire another signal projectile through a familiar
pair of mouths.

## Mechanic progression

1. **Fuel is rhythm.** Bellows Rest gives enough height to feel capable, then
   requires a visible landing before the next lift. Fuel never runs dry over a
   blind lethal void.
2. **Air belongs to the place.** Kite Stair uses devices and cloth indicators to
   show bounds and direction before entry. Player and eligible enemies sample the
   same field.
3. **Air accepts elements.** One optional encounter exposes existing fire, ice,
   and storm reactions. Fire can make a current dangerous, ice can reduce it, and
   storm can intensify it; required progression has a neutral solution.
4. **Air can be repaired.** Rain-Catcher links multiple vanes and Ilyra to a
   persistent service-current state. The task spans the room and changes traversal.
5. **Air can be redirected.** Signal Crown projects the existing crosswind through
   a player-owned portal pair. The receiver responds to sustained correctly faced
   airflow, not a projectile collision or one-frame overlap.

## Portal and decoy grammar

- Both mouths are placed by the player. The intake surface sits inside or beside
  the readable crosswind; candidate exits face the Crown at distinct heights and
  orientations.
- Projected particles, moving sail cloth, and audio show flow direction before the
  receiver commits. The game does not solve orientation with tutorial text.
- The correct mouth pair sustains the Crown windwheel and opens the gate
  persistently. Passing the player or an enemy through the pair is permitted but
  is not the activation condition.
- Decoys are honest surfaces with consequences, not dead controls. A low outlet
  assists an encounter or exposes recovery; a misaligned high outlet lowers a
  ladder. Neither destroys the portal pair, traps a player, or secretly progresses
  the gate.
- Checkpoint, death, reload, revisit, and co-op snapshot convergence must agree on
  vane repair, Crown charge, door state, and player mouths. A stale projected
  field may never survive without its pair.

## Environmental and visual contract

- Every platform must read as canyon rock, scaffold, maintenance walk, cistern
  masonry, or Signal Crown hardware and must have visible support or suspension.
- Thermals originate at vents/bellows and crosswinds are bounded by flags, dust,
  vegetation, and pipe/sail movement. Environmental fields are never placed only
  to increase their count.
- The Rain-Catcher is the only water feature. It requires a containing floor and
  side walls, a visible inlet/overflow, a safe exit, and an audit-marked basin.
- Wind-reactive enemies receive authored patrol bounds and catch ledges. They may
  fall between platforms while chasing, but the environment cannot clear them in
  an offscreen ground-floor pit before contact.
- Room silhouettes differ at a glance: low shelter and bellows; narrow rising kite
  lines; broad pale cistern masonry; then the tall Crown and radial sails.
- Reduced-motion mode preserves field boundaries, direction, reactions, and
  receiver state without relying on screen shake or dense particles.

## Narrative and people charter

- **Ilyra, Windwright** remains the stage traveler but no longer waits beside a
  nearby brass wind-vane. She is stranded beyond the damaged Rain-Catcher current;
  restoring its separated controls and reaching her emits `traveler-helped:ilyra`
  and begins **Kindling the Sky**. She then persists at her work station for the
  later global return objective.
- Add at most two incidental residents in `L04-C`: a kite-mender at Bellows Rest
  and a rain-catcher keeper at the cistern. They need distinct silhouettes,
  occupations, first/revisit dialogue, and no follower or vendor behavior.
- There is no shop. Ethereal Goods in Black Woods is one safe visited-world return
  away; another counter would flatten regional identity and pacing.
- Racing pulse is conveyed indirectly: wind skips a beat, the harness straps seem
  to answer the knight's chest, and Ilyra counts a rhythm that the machinery does
  not share. Nobody diagnoses poisoning or delirium.
- The global `third-note` record belongs on a maintained upper perch with a view
  of the Crown. The searcher's `clean-bandage` belongs on a reachable recovery
  shelf, implying someone survived a fall and continued forward.
- Sparse white trumpet flowers grow only in sheltered cracks where the main wind
  cannot plausibly carry them. They remain noncollectible and unexplained.
- This stage adds no major memory. Its job is environmental and symptomatic
  reinforcement so later flashbacks retain weight.
