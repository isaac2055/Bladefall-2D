## Discoveries and comparisons

### B50 — Close the Receiving Mouth

The transport opening is initially the reason the delivery will not stay delivered. Destroying the link restores the piece of architecture that the belt needs as its stop.

Compared with existing rooms: Open the Other End (F20, banked in Base) repositions a portal to change which side of a stone can be reached; B20 preserves a closed door as a ladder. This room closes its useful transport link to turn the receiving wall into a conveyor stop, with the same weight then maintaining the exit.

Ending: After the lantern parks, both leaves stay open and the exit is an ordinary broad walk. The two visibly separate doors prevent dashing through the fleeting plate contact while the portal remains linked. Returned cargo rests visibly on the western catch shelf rather than looping.

### B51 — Make the Watch Look Away

The safe approach and the useful departure require opposite states of the same wall. Preparation happens in a sheltered pocket with a visible thaw window, and the seed supplies a high route over the watch cone that an ordinary jump cannot reach.

Compared with existing rooms: B36 uses an impulse seed to preserve speed into a portal, while F18 turns frozen track into a later cart descent. Here temporary ice creates the concealment needed to prepare the seed but is itself the obstacle to the outgoing launch; liquid water softens the landing and a second smoke pocket receives it.

### B52 — Send the Bolt Upwind

The shared passage has three different uses at three heights: a bolt travels upwind through the empty frame, a carrier takes the knight below the thorn-covered firing lane, and the anvil returns the knight above that same lane into the casting pocket. Only then can the frame become the screen needed for the long downwind home route.

Compared with existing rooms: B29 combines transported shade with a returned shot, B30 casts shade during a swing, and B12 replaces a temporary shield. Here a shot can cross the gale that prevents the knight from reaching the casting face. Its coil releases both the lower carriage and the home door; a lower ride and upper anvil return approach the same frame from its useful side before the final walk uses its permanent shadow.

Shared structure: The mould stands in the outgoing shot line and the returning watch-light line. One distant coil opens the exit beside the starting station and releases the lower carrier. The carrier delivers the knight to the anvil beyond the thorn field; that anvil reaches the high return gallery, which drops onto the windward casting face. The final gust returns to the original parry platform and home door.

### B53 — What the Tide Leaves Behind

The water changes which face of the heavy object is reachable. The stone stays where the knight sent it after the enabling water has gone, so its second job survives the tide. The tall hinged partition becomes a reusable crossing, and the float can be left behind once it has delivered the knight above swimming height.

Compared with existing rooms: B13 manufactures a stopper for a horizontal block; B12 trades a beam shield for a bridge; the original Landing Shores rooms use tidal floats for launch timing. This room uses submerged thorns to permit access below a full-width sluice stone. Its upward displacement simultaneously opens the wet outward route and leaves a permanent dry stair for a return directly above that route. No lantern transport or optical circuit is involved.

Shared structure: Two western passages are vertically stacked around one well: a flooded thorn tunnel outward and a dry upper exit home. Moving the same visible stone connects these paths. The tide and float govern access to a persistent hinged opening, which the knight traverses in both directions.

Ending: The return stone offers a stable rest before a 16-pixel rise into a 32-tile sheltered gallery. A three-second rest on the stone and a two-frame earlier sideways input both finish without deaths. The far tally balcony is eight tiles wide; missing its anvil transfer drops to a broad safe yard. No final swing, moving catch or momentum chain is required.

### F51 — Wait for the Arrow

Recovering on the fall and restoring the bridge is only the arrival problem. The same portal subsequently carries a shot, and the useful landing has become the last obstruction in its path. The correct second use of the bridge is to leave it behind while withdrawing it; the final bank is safe while the shot completes its route.

Compared with existing rooms: F47 also restores an aerial phase landing; F13 spends a useful surface to open a later optical route. This room combines a direction-sensitive falling recovery with an opposing second use of that exact phase bridge: it must first catch the knight, then disappear as a projectile shutter. The bridge tally physically opens the shot feed only after arrival, so simply leaving the bridge absent cannot solve both jobs.

Ending: The last dash leaves the old bridge for a broad solid bank. The receiver fires repeatedly and there is no final aim window or catch; wait safely at the door if the next shot has not arrived.

### F52 — Put It on the Other Floor

Preparing the launch object and preparing the player use different floors. Inversion changes the approach without transporting the floating object to a new height; the earlier blade direction remains visible in the eventual launch clearance.

Compared with existing rooms: B36 places a launch beside a portal, F36 revisits a seed from its other side, and B14 uses inversion to deliver cargo. Here gravity changes access to a seed that must retain the correct absolute height for a thorn-lined launch opening.

Ending: The second strike is performed from a stable foothold, not during a jump. Ten- and twenty-frame extra waits and a one-frame approach change finish without deaths. The 14-tile receiving ceiling supports a long safe walk to the exit; no late swing, dash or catch is required.

### F53 — Let It Through First

The knight manufactures a return structure from the route that transported the object. Freezing first prevents delivery; leaving it liquid removes the dry wall and exposes the approach to a beam. The later object reunion supplies the easy shaded finish, so the hard movement happens before the final reward.

Compared with existing rooms: F14 Empty Your Hands separates cargo and knight through a one-tile water duct, while F18 The Thawing Track changes ice support to relocate useful shade. This room keeps one connected pool and asks it to do opposing jobs: liquid delivers the lantern, then the frozen outlet becomes the knight’s beam shield and wallbounce face. The dock launch draws on B39’s stored lift, but its useful destination exists only after the earlier cargo route is deliberately closed.

Shared structure: The down/across/up pool is both the complete cargo route and the later frozen climbing structure. The lantern delivered through it is the shade needed beyond that structure.

Ending: Both late ascent transfers have grounded rests, and a missed wallbounce can fall back onto the lower shelf. The final lantern glide has a high, open receiving boundary and a broad bank, with no last precision catch. Ice lasts sixty seconds after the condenser; the recorded route uses about eleven seconds of that interval.

### F54 — Open the Fall

The first passenger through the portal changes the physical journey available to its second passenger. A closed lid is simultaneously a carrier stop and a height limit; opening it creates both the approach and its stored fall momentum.

Compared with existing rooms: F28 Reply in Passing and B29 The Shield You Send route converted bolts through portals, while B36 Short Runway, Tall Exit prepares a useful entrance velocity. Here the bolt is not a separate lock at the end: its delivery removes the actual horizontal lid which traps the carrier and caps the later falling runway. The same outlet first carries the opening shot, then the knight whose launch the shot made possible.

Shared structure: One orange outlet serves both deliveries. The first delivery removes the shared carrier obstruction and fall ceiling needed for the second.

Ending: The final bank is ten tiles wide and requires no last dash or wall catch. Carrier departure works earlier and later, and floor-portal placement can be delayed substantially. The inward teeth mark the low wall-catch shortcut while leaving the intended landing surface clear.

**B50 — Close the Receiving Mouth:** Place the blue mouth in the marked western floor, collect the lantern, and carry it into the link. After arriving on the eastern belt, walk left through the grill while still carrying the weight. Closing the blue link restores the orange opening as solid wall. Return right, stop steering and set the lantern down onto the belt. It now parks against the former mouth on the pressure plate instead of returning to the start. Jump over that wall and continue through the held-open door. The two separated door leaves need a sustained weight: the belt’s brief passing contact cannot hold the whole exit passage open.

**B51 — Make the Watch Look Away:** Strike the condenser from the starting smoke to freeze the water column. Its ice blocks the watch light while you cross the exposed gap to the inner smoke. Pass the seed, face left and strike it back into that shelter. Stand a little to its right and let the ice thaw; jumping and cutting down onto the prepared seed now sends you over the watch cone and through the liquid column into the receiving smoke. **B52 — Send the Bolt Upwind:** From the smoke, parry a bolt right through the still-empty casting frame into the distant coil. That opens the home door and the lower carriage route, but home is still exposed to the watch light. Drop onto the broad carrier and ride beneath the gale and the thorn floor to the far side. Jump onto the wide anvil pedestal and land before using a small jump and downward cut. Its rebound reaches the high gallery above the same thorn and projectile lane. Follow the gale into the sheltered shaft, let yourself settle beside the mould, and strike left to cast both parts of the screen. Jump left over the new low wall; the central gust carries you across the gap to the starting smoke. Walk home through the screen’s long shadow.

**B53 — What the Tide Leaves Behind:** Begin at full tide and cross the submerged thorn reef. In the narrow sluice well, swim beneath the broad stone and dash upward into it. It parks against the visible overhead stop, opening the side passage into the basin. Approach the float from its side, climb onto it and jump to the gallery above normal swimming height. Strike the hinged partition at its hub; the former wall becomes a horizontal walk into the eastern yard. Hop and cut down onto the broad anvil to reach the tally balcony. Return across the turned arm. The sea has now exposed the lower reef, so drop onto the same stone that you raised out of the well, pause there and jump into the upper western gallery. The final walk is sheltered and level.

**F51 — Wait for the Arrow:** Make a short hop in the low western throat, then dash left into the portal while airborne. The outlet turns the remaining dash upward through the floor your dash removed. Let the rise finish: the down-arrow crystal rejects the ascent and restores the dash on the fall. Dash right after that recovery to restore the phase bridge under you. Touch its visible tally: this opens the gun feed below, but the bridge now blocks the shot. Jump and dash off the bridge onto the broad eastern bank. Removing your old landing lets the converted portal shot reach the sealed receiver and open the last door; walk out when it retracts.

**F52 — Put It on the Other Floor:** From the original floor, strike the seed upward into the narrow passage's absolute height. Dash through the prism to turn the room upside down. Jump left onto the small stable tooth beside the charged seed, then strike left while standing: the seed throws you right through the thorn-lined slit. The old floor briefly limits the arc overhead. Land on the broad far ceiling and walk out. The seed's position survives the gravity change; preparing it after the flip would move it in the wrong world direction.

**F53 — Let It Through First:** Throw the lantern into the western water intake and let the current carry it down, through the one-tile conduit, and up to the high clasp. Once delivery is visible, strike the condenser. That same connected pool now blocks the crossing beam and becomes the solid intake and upper return wall. Climb the intake, jump from the small stone sill onto the dock, hold Down to load it, then release and jump with its rise. Dash onto the broad shelf below the frozen outlet. Dash upward beside the ice and hold Jump to bounce onto the higher outboard rest; a short rising transfer and climb reaches the crown. Reclaim the lantern from its clasp and take the wide, shaded glide across the final beams.

**F54 — Open the Fall:** Place the blue portal on the exposed western shot wall. A hostile bolt enters it and emerges friendly from the distant orange mouth, reaching the sealed receiver. Advance to the carrier and get aboard; the receiver retracts the lid which had stopped its upward rail. Ride to the high staging ledge. Move over the marked well, relocate the blue portal to its floor, and fall into it. The same orange outlet now turns your long drop into the range needed to clear the moat and land on the wide eastern bank. The reopened lid was necessary for both the ride and that fall.

## Evidence and limits

All 86 active canonical routes finish with zero deaths. Saved checks also cover 371 successful variations and 214 specific bypass probes across the bank, including 23 variations and 24 probes for Run 6. Every new room is saved individually with a complete route. Browser checks cover all eight new rooms, camera travel through the large maps, menu groups, review submission, stable revision IDs, CSV export and the play timer/pause. Layouts and intermediate scrolling views have been inspected.

These checks establish feasibility and the named regression protections. They do not measure fun or prove that every alternate route has been ruled out. Human Fun /10, Difficulty /5 and Suitability /5 remain unmeasured. Reviews accept an optional comment and unfinished attempts. Play mode omits the survey and retains its timer.
