## What to review

The full synthesis goal is a useful interaction between familiar and newer systems. This set reaches beyond the previous lantern/echo/carrier routes: track choices, landing-triggered machinery, approach-dependent launches, reusable enemies, travelling fault lines and companion path memory. The larger rooms make earlier choices matter again on a return route. Fractured endings have broad receiving areas or safe final galleries; difficulty estimates still need human measurement.

## Discoveries and comparisons

### B46 — Let the Train Go Low

The normally helpful high-track switch is the wrong destination for the vehicle: the knight deliberately parts from the cart so it supplies shade below the feather flight.

Compared with existing rooms: F18 uses one cart for speed and a later shadow; B15 separates a knight and a useful object. Here the branch itself asks you to leave the vehicle: its low destination supplies shade while the knight takes the feather route above.

### B47 — Two Landings, One Light

Landing is a spatial control input: the same lift that takes you to a mirror temporarily obstructs the circuit it will later power. The lift return also removes the optical obstruction.

Compared with existing rooms: B12 and B31 change a route by redirecting light and revisiting the same structure. This room instead couples the transport floor directly to the remote obstruction: its first landing raises access but interrupts the receiver, and its second landing restores the light.

### B48 — The Train Comes Back Empty

Unlike F18, the cart has a piloted high-fork journey, a separate thawed arrival, and an intentional reversal from the newly accessible eastern face. The optical completion removes the outgoing bridge, so the return must be redrawn below it; the same train creates that lower shadow.

Compared with existing rooms: F18 is the nearest cart/thaw/shadow room, and B29 sends a movable shield ahead. This switchyard adds a piloted high fork, an optical latch that physically removes the outgoing rail, access to the train’s far face after thaw, and an explicit reversal that shades the lower return.

Shared structure: One train occupies both routes in opposite directions. The upper optical latch opens the home exit and simultaneously removes the outward rail; freezing temporarily catches the train, thawing gives access to its east face, and reversing it creates the return shadow below the missing bridge.

### B49 — The Rooms Trade Their Floors

The recoil spring is a shared origin with two different destinations. Landing changes three remote pieces of geometry at once, so the ascent temporarily obstructs its own exit circuit. A permanent hinge conversion creates an upper return bridge, while the opposite relay landing completes the prepared light circuit and makes the homeward trip possible.

Compared with existing rooms: B35 supplies the reusable recoil lift, B11/B12 supply a climbing arm that becomes a bridge, and B31 supplies a changed optical return. Here two opposed lifts and their shared optical shutter trade positions together: the first landing grants ascent, the far landing completes a circuit and returns the near lift below.

Shared structure: Both lifts and the optical shutter share one landing relay. Raising the near lift lowers the far dock and blocks the receiver; landing on that far dock reverses all three after the mirror is prepared. The hinge is first climbed and then crossed both ways as a bridge. The same Home spring launches toward the two different lower destinations before and after the circuit is solved.

### F47 — Come From the Wrong Side

The apparent destination is a low island directly beneath a bridge that cannot be reached from a normal dash. The solution first moves away from the exit, approaching the pressure pod from its far side so its recoil returns both useful direction and a fresh dash. The final blade input changes the same bridge just crossed, and the landing is seven tiles wide followed by the whole exit floor.

Compared with existing rooms: F34 uses a wallbounce and an aerial phase bell. This chamber instead makes the approach direction to a pressure pod and the parity of two successive dashes inseparable: the return launch must clear an island lip, spend a recovered dash through the bridge, then restore the bridge with the blade.

Ending: Seven-tile receiving bridge continues into a full safe exit gallery. The final bell cut and pressure-pod approach both tolerate several consecutive input timings.

### F48 — Bring the Ram Down

A floor changes not only the knight route but the enemy route. The ram must first be delivered to another altitude; the knight then reaches its opposite side to use it again. The folded floor becomes the ascent supported by that same ram.

Compared with existing rooms: B27 lets a ram replace one shield; B11 makes a bridge become a stair. Here transforming the bridge transports a living breaching tool into an otherwise sealed chamber, and approach side reverses its useful job.

Ending: The launch ends on a six-tile one-way balcony. Missing the ascent lands on the solid arena floor; the living ram can be parried and used again. A full rest, refill and ordinary climb separate the enemy launch from the eleven-tile exit gallery. No final wallbounce or timed catch is required.

### F49 — Open It on the Way Out

The outgoing road is also the obstruction of the route underneath it. Crossing the entire upper deck prepares both the lower wall opening and the final upward return, while actual tallies make the east descent consequential. The lower route changes the movement question from keeping ahead of the crack to chaining a lofted pogo, a suspended refill target and a feather recovery. The ending is the resolved return to the place visible at spawn.

Compared with existing rooms: F13 changes a later return by spending an earlier surface; F18 uses a changing track and F42 chains deliberate blade launches. This foundry ties those decisions to one connected fault line: the upper road, middle return wall and ceiling above the final anvil are physically the same crack network.

Shared structure: Touching the upper fault deck starts the connected crack down through the lower return wall. That same deck is the ceiling above the final anvil; its disappearance permits the return launch. The outbound east tally and lower return tally open the visible gate beside the original entrance.

Ending: Two reusable anvils and a nine-tile one-way rest shelf finish on an eleven-tile entrance balcony. Missing either final jump drops to safe ground; the cracked route stays open for a full minute. A three-second rest before the last launch remains valid.

### F50 — Walk the Answer Twice

Oren cannot be placed remotely or teleported: the knight authors the helper path before requesting the hold. Keeping the convenient passage open is exactly what prevents the return launch. The east tally opens a visibly gated upper approach, preventing an initial hop over the helper puzzle; the pier’s west teeth prevent climbing the outbound side. The useful new recoil platform and late wallbounce only work together after the old companion interaction changes shared geometry.

Compared with existing rooms: F19 asks that a weight stay while the knight leaves, and F34 makes a wall useful only in one machinery state. B39 (Borrow Only Enough) supplies the compress–release–jump timing of the recoil platform. Here the helper must first copy a real descent to a distant plate, then be explicitly called off it after the lower crossing. The same central pier changes from obstruction to open passage to recoil-assisted wallbounce face to upper return support.

Shared structure: The knight must really visit the middle pressure shelf before Oren can replay that path and hold it. The helper opens the central pier for the outward lower crossing, then must leave the same plate to restore it as the recoil-assisted return wall. The restored pier also supports the upper return across the same room to the original entrance gate.

Ending: The recoil-to-wallbounce transfer has clean early and late variants and lands on an eleven-tile one-way rest. Missing it drops to a safe local floor beside the reusable recoil plate. From that rest onward, the return is a climb, a broad upper shelf and an ordinary jump to the twelve-tile entrance balcony. A three-second pause on the middle return shelf remains valid.

## Evidence and limits

All 78 active canonical routes complete with zero deaths. The saved checks also cover 348 positive variations and 190 specific shortcut probes across the bank. Each new room is independently saved with its own tape. These checks establish feasibility and the listed regression protections, not measured fun or proof against every possible alternate route.

Browser checks cover all eight Run 5 rooms, real camera travel in the large maps, review submission, stable revision IDs, CSV export, play timer and pause behavior. Rail and water parsing, collision and drawing now respect the authored room dimensions; focused tests exercise the same interactions inside and outside the original viewport. Ordinary movement rules are unchanged.

All 70 previous records are preserved byte-for-byte, including the four earlier superrooms and their current review status. The bank remains 46 approved rooms. The new eight are candidates, with no invented human ratings. Review mode asks Fun /10, Difficulty /5 and Suitability /5, with an optional comment; unfinished attempts can also be rated. Play mode omits the survey and retains the timer.
