# The next ten Survive superrooms — proposal, 7.304

This is an authoring plan, not ten implemented rooms. The ten approved existing superrooms are now campaign finales in worlds 8, 12, 15, 18, 19, 24, 26, 32, 35 and 38. That leaves 30 worlds without a superroom. This next set fills **five Base and five Fractured** slots; six proposals include that world’s boss.

The most useful next set is not ten harder endurance rooms. It adds two early Base finales, one middle Base portal puzzle, two Base boss syntheses, three Fractured boss syntheses, one late Fractured timing return, and the Fractured Palace finale. The common requirement is a space whose first actions alter the later route. Difficulty comes from decisions and combinations; the last jump must have a generous receiving platform and recovery space where the fiction permits it.

Boss proposals replace the named **last boss challenge**, keeping earlier boss lessons, NPCs, quest branches, interworld exits and individual checkpoints intact. They are new continuous arenas, not three existing rooms joined under a single checkpoint. Where the Palace ends with a narrative ride, that ride remains a non-scored departure passage after the new final boss challenge. No Vesper or Echo ending is included in this Survive batch; that remains deferred until the 40 superroom finales are finished.

## 1 — The Gate Remembers Your Weight

A low central gate divides a broad courtyard. A lantern travels on a slow lower carrier beneath the same upper platforms the knight traverses. First, walk with the carrier while a patrol crosses the upper notch; step off at the recess to let it deliver the weight onto the plate. The gate opens the return crossing, not the exit. Climb the gate’s exposed frame, cross its upper gallery and retrieve the lantern from the other side. Removing the weight closes the gate into a stair leading to the exit above the original fire.

The first route teaches every role at ground level before asking for the return. Only basic jump, dash, grab/carry and patrol observation: no blade, portal or advanced launch. Two wide waiting bays, a harmless floor below the first missed jump, and a final ordinary jump from a stationary stair. The critical shortcut check is reaching the upper exit before removing the weight. No new mechanic needed.

## 4 — Leave the Wind a Way Home

A central wind shaft serves both a knight and a shade lantern. Depart with the lantern through a beam, set it on a high shelf to open a low door, then descend the now-open windward side with empty hands. A pod rebound reaches the draft’s opposite ledge, where a blade-operated hinged vane changes which side receives the wind. The final return picks the lantern up from above and rides the altered draft into the sheltered exit gallery.

The vane is visible from the starting chamber and changes the same shaft, not a separate last obstacle. The hard decision is when to give up cargo glide. Keep the final landing at least a full platform wide; a mistimed return should fall into the sheltered lower pocket rather than restart the whole room. No grapple, portal or advanced launch. Check both “keep the lantern throughout” and “climb directly up the exit wall.”

## 6 — The Cargo Takes the Shortcut

Two portal mouths cross a barred storage shaft. The knight can use one mouth, but a carried lantern cannot pass the low receiving duct. Send it alone to create shade on the far platform, close that receiving mouth, and take a longer moving-platform route. Recover the lantern from the other side, then send a sliding stone through the now-reopened portal. The stone displaces the lantern onto the exit plate while becoming the knight’s final landing.

This develops B50 and F20: closing a portal must matter, and the player and cargo take different routes through the same well. The receiving ledge stays broad and visible. The final stone is stationary once parked. Check portals left open, a direct carry, and a dash to the exit before the stone’s delivery. No mandatory hyper or wallbounce.

## 11 — Cast Your Escape

The Colossus pours into three connected bays around one central overhead rail. The first blocked pour creates a shield that is useful twice: it protects the knight from the next hunting pour and gives an anvil a new stopping point. The second bay asks the player to send the anvil beneath the boss, then pogo it into the elevated casting side. The third pour must strike the now-vacated mould; that opens a broad lower passage leading back beneath the original shield and out of the arena.

The boss attacks create or remove useful architecture. Do not make a last-frame dodge the entire ending: telegraph the hunting pour clearly, provide a full-width sheltered bay after the anvil transfer, and keep the final exit behind stable cast iron. Preserve the existing Colossus’s recognizable tells and defeat state. Check any route that blocks all pours from one spot or bypasses the anvil using a wall climb.

## 20 — Give the Sun Back

The King’s beam travels through a central optical court. First use a movable shade body to reach a portal face; returning one beam disables the low lane and raises a platform into a useful wall. The next circuit uses that wall to reach the high mouth while the shade body must be left below. The final reflection uses the original starting mirror from its opposite side, opening a floor route through the King’s former attack lane.

The first two existing King encounters teach beam language; this finale combines beam return, moving shade and portal geometry in one readable court. All Base movement. Provide safe cover before each optical decision, and let the last reflected beam resolve from a stable platform. Keep the hydra’s Sea Terrace departure and dialogue after the victory. Check any stationary one-portal solution that defeats every stage without changing height or shade placement.

## 21 — Ring It from the Other Side

The Sentinel’s hammer becomes the outward launch platform and later the receiving surface. A super off the first hammer arc reaches a side balcony. Ringing a low bell moves its next strike to the opposite half of the arena, leaving a stationary rest shelf where the hammer was. Cross the sweep from that shelf, use the returning hammer’s lift for the second super, then strike the high bell from behind the arena’s central column.

Use only the super lesson taught in World 21: no required hyper, ultra or wallbounce before their native introductions. Make the first bell’s effect observable from safety. End with a wide high balcony rather than a second precise midair cut. Preserve the Sentinel defeat transition; check early high-bell access and any single hammer ride that bypasses the bell relocation.

## 25 — His Next Perch Is Your Landing

The Marksman’s arrows open temporary cover, and his next perch is reached by the knight before he takes it. Parry a low shot from the outgoing carrier; use the refill to land on his empty middle perch. Its weight switch redirects the carrier beneath his next firing lane. Drop onto that returning carrier, keep the borrowed speed through a wavedash, and return the final paired shot while crossing toward a broad exit shelf.

This follows F33’s preferred “parry, retreat, wavedash” route and F27’s moving passenger. It must stay portal-free because World 25’s kit is portal-free. The final shot gets a broad parry window and a usable fallback shelf; preserving momentum gives the elegant route, but a tiny aiming error must not demand restarting every earlier exchange. Check a wall-cling parry camp and stationary returns from the first platform.

## 29 — Thaw the Way Behind Him

The Sorcerer’s frost initially creates a bridge the knight needs, but retaining that bridge blocks the returning shot. Cross on the first freeze, use the far anvil to reach the casting alcove, and deliberately thaw the middle bridge. The next frost cast travels through the empty lane to create an upper landing behind the boss. Cross a mirror boundary only after that landing is established, then return his final cast from the original bridge’s former support.

The same water serves as travel, obstruction and later a landing. Keep the freeze/thaw cause visible rather than hide it behind an offscreen timer. Include a safe alcove after each state change and a wide final casting ledge. Check “never thaw,” “freeze every surface immediately,” and a direct dash to the upper perch before the second cast.

## 37 — The Tide Keeps the Appointment

The outbound path follows a slow tidal float through two differently shaped patrol paths. The knight leaves the float for a low launch through a dust-bunny opening, while a lantern remains aboard and passes safely through a cargo duct. A hinge cut in the upper gallery opens an underpass for the return tide. Drop onto the returning float, intercept the lantern from above, and use its shade through the original exposed bay toward the exit over the fire.

This extends F55/F56’s continuous timing without making the last move harder than everything before it. Patrols should combine one stationary cluster, one curved path and one low horizontal sweep; do not draw their trajectories. Safe pockets let the player learn the cycle. The final lantern interception has a broad deck and the exit is a modest hop after a restable landing. Test missed-cycle recovery and whether the player can keep the lantern through the outbound underpass.

## 40 — The Shadow Makes the Last Bridge

The final King arena has one shared carrier, a high portal mouth and a lower echo plate. The outward traversal uses the carrier as shade. Leave an echo at the first station, ride onward and redirect one beam so the carrier becomes a moving wall for a wallbounce. That reaches the upper address without the cargo. Swap back to retrieve the shade lantern, then take the changed lower route; the echo remains at the upper station and holds the final gate while the player returns the last beam through the same portal face.

Develop F32’s reverse-launch cargo handling and F57’s timing, while recalling F45/F46’s strongest shared-state ideas. Keep the last reflection on a generous stationary shelf. Echo placement and the final launch must have independent tolerances; do not require one position that barely satisfies both. Preserve the current King’s defeat/Sea Terrace transition. This is not the deferred Vesper encounter. Check direct climbing to the upper station, leaving cargo at the first plate, and an echo placement that accidentally bypasses the second traversal.
