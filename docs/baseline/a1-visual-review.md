# A1 visual review

## Evidence boundary

This review covers 137 camera-positioned stills captured from the canonical
version `7.8.0` build at `http://127.0.0.1:8372/`. The atlas includes all 16
assembled stages and is indexed at:

`docs/baseline/evidence/index.html`

The capture used a fresh isolated save, invulnerability, and camera positioning.
It is evidence of assembled appearance and content distribution. It does not
prove that a route, puzzle, follower, or boss can be completed normally.

## Campaign-wide findings

### 1. Biomes are palettes more often than places

The background palette and silhouettes distinguish forest, frost, ember, ruin,
dungeon, and void themes. Foreground construction rarely explains where the
player is, who lived there, what a space was used for, or why its mechanisms
exist. Ruined Keep does not visibly form a keep; Emberdeep does not form a forge
district; Frostfell does not form a settlement; boss approaches generally remain
flat obstacle streams.

### 2. Repeated obstacle language overwhelms regional identity

Long flat slabs, black pits, floating shelves, hanging blocks, isolated spike
rows, narrow wall columns, crystals, and portal markers recur across most
regions. The pieces can create mechanical variety but make the campaign feel
assembled from the same kit in a different color.

### 3. Environmental systems lack physical construction

The reported Updrafts water problem is visible: a blue water rectangle sits on
top of ordinary ground without recessed terrain or containing shores.
Emberdeep's relay furnace similarly presents a rectangular lava volume without
a convincing furnace basin. Ember Colossus's coolant reads as a large floating
blue block rather than a tank, channel, cistern, or pipe-fed machine.

These are not art-polish defects. Geometry and system placement must be
re-authored together.

### 4. Much of the camera is unused

Many views devote most of the screen to empty sky while the playable route
occupies a thin strip near the bottom. Occasional floating platforms do not
create a coherent vertical route. This is especially evident in Outskirts,
Ruined Keep, Frostfell, the elemental boss approaches, and the void finale.

### 5. Mechanisms are readable but detached

Portal mouths, rune devices, moving siphons, boss phase pillars, crown markers,
pendulums, gravity fields, and relays are often visually legible. Their immediate
function can be understood, but they commonly appear as devices placed on a
runway rather than machinery belonging to the surrounding architecture.

### 6. The strongest current spaces use continuous geometry

Gilded Vault has the clearest authored platforming cadence: flame lanes, needle
landings, a shaft, and moving catches form a continuous exam. Deep Line's rail
topography also reads as a journey rather than scattered chunks. Both still need
stronger narrative and architectural identity, but they are useful structural
references for later planning.

## Priority findings for later charters

1. Rebuild the campaign from connected rooms and physical places, not from a
   longer catalog of obstacle chunks.
2. Treat foreground architecture, system physics, NPC function, and narrative
   meaning as one room-design problem.
3. Use Gilded Vault's continuous challenge grammar and Deep Line's continuous
   topography as structural references, not as visual templates.
4. Require every fluid, wind field, moving platform, portal surface, and gravity
   field to have visible construction and a reason to exist.
5. Budget vertical screen space deliberately. Empty sky is useful only when it
   creates scale, anticipation, navigation information, or aerial gameplay.
6. Preserve the readable boss mechanisms while rebuilding their approaches and
   arenas as narrative locations.

## Remaining A1 work

- Dynamic recordings of system activation, failure, reset, and success.
- Intended, optional, revisit, speedrun, and co-op route playthroughs.
- Interactive boss-phase and death/checkpoint verification.
- Runtime-coordinate correction for any upper-layer still that camera
  positioning placed on an unproven support.

A1 therefore remains open even though its structural inventory and static visual
atlas are complete.
